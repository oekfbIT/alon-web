AI Judge for Card Games

An iPhone camera follows a trading card game, an AI agent checks whether plays are legal, keeps score and talks to the players.

Overview

A judge for trading card games, starting with Yu-Gi-Oh!. An iPhone on a tripod films the table from above, and an AI follows the duel the way a human judge would: what was played, what attacked and what that did to the life points.

A screen next to the table shows both players' life points, updated as the game happens, together with a duel log of every summon, set card, attack and effect. A vision model reads the cards on the mat and the system keeps the game state in order, so the numbers on screen come from what actually happened.

Players can also just talk to it. Ask whether a play is allowed and the judge answers by voice with a ruling and the rule behind it. It is a personal project in progress; the preview shows a concept with invented cards and sample data.

How a duel runs

  1. Watch. The phone on the tripod films the mat from above and the vision model finds cards, zones and their positions.
  2. Track. Every summon, set card, attack and graveyard move goes into the duel log, and the life points update on screen.
  3. Check. Each play is checked against the rules, for example one Normal Summon per turn, and marked as legal or not.
  4. Rule. Players ask by voice and get a spoken ruling with the reason, plus a note in the log.

What I built

  • Designing the camera setup: one iPhone on a tripod, no special mat or sensors.
  • Detecting cards, zones and face-up or face-down state with a vision model.
  • Keeping a game state and duel log, so life points and rulings come from tracked events.
  • Adding voice, so players can ask the judge questions in the middle of a duel.
