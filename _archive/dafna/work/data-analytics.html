Data before opinions: using product and operational data to drive decisions

Data analysis is part of my normal product workflow, not a separate activity. The goal isn’t producing dashboards; it’s deciding what to do differently.

Overview

Product decisions are easy to make on opinion. The aim is to connect product analytics to a concrete business decision.

How it works

  1. Business question
  2. Data: SQL, Metabase, PostHog, Salesforce, DWH
  3. Insight
  4. Decision
  5. Measure again

Conceptual illustration.

My role

Tools and sources I work with include SQL, Metabase, PostHog, Salesforce reporting, data warehouse data, CSV and Excel analysis, and product dashboards. I also use AI to support analysis, ideation, structuring and visualisation where appropriate.

How I approached it

Start from the decision, then find the data that informs it, and validate the result against what the business and the systems actually do.

Key decisions

  • Decisions, not dashboardsAn analysis is finished when it changes what we do next.

Technical and operational complexity

Questions I investigate

  • Where are customers dropping out of the booking funnel?
  • Why are certain payments failing?
  • How are individual locations performing?
  • Which storage sizes have excess capacity?
  • Are promotions increasing competitiveness?
  • Which Customer Service topics occur most frequently?
  • Which support cases could potentially be automated?
  • How successful was a newly launched product?
  • Which customer behaviours should influence pricing?

Outcome

Book in Advance is a good example. A measurement plan defined before launch made it possible to evaluate reservation usage, booking realisation and commercial contribution, and to decide whether the product deserved further investment.

Read the Book in Advance case study

What I learned

  • The most useful analysis is usually the one that answers a question someone was about to decide on opinion.
