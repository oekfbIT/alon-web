Overview

When a customer doesn’t pay, the process is more complicated than sending another email. Countries have different legal requirements around notice periods, fees, compensation, communication, deadlines and debt-collection handover.

My role

I have worked on country-specific dunning logic for Storebox markets, including workflows involving Pair Finance, with examples in the Netherlands, Belgium and Switzerland. For the Netherlands this included the statutory 14-day consumer notice process and the collection handling that follows.

How I approached it

The product has to prevent incorrect debt-collection handovers while automating as much of the process as possible. That involves timers, invoice and payment states, communication templates, legal rules, external provider integrations, auditability and exception handling.

Key decisions

  • Re-check before every stepPayment status is checked again before acting, because a customer may have paid after the reminder was generated.
  • Exceptions stop the automationDisputed invoices leave the automated path instead of being handed over.
  • Make it auditableEach step has to be traceable, because these are regulated processes with real consequences for customers.

Technical and operational complexity

Questions the workflow has to answer

  • Is the outstanding balance still open?
  • Has the customer paid since the reminder was generated?
  • Is the invoice disputed?
  • Should additional fees be applied?
  • Is the customer eligible for debt-collection handover?
  • Has the legally required waiting period passed?

Outcome

Dunning logic that encodes country-specific legal requirements and protects customers from incorrect handovers, while keeping the process automated.

What I learned

  • In regulated workflows, the most important feature is often the one that stops the automation at the right moment.
