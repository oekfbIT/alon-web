From Customer Need to New Booking Product

Turning early customer demand into a new self-service booking journey, then validating whether it created real customer and commercial value.

Overview

The existing Storebox booking journey was primarily designed for customers who needed storage immediately.

But not every customer searches at the moment they are ready to move in. Some start looking days or weeks earlier and want certainty that a suitable unit will still be available when they need it.

That created a product opportunity: instead of forcing those customers to either book immediately or leave, could we give them a simple way to reserve a unit for a future start date?

My role was to help turn that need into a product concept, define the MVP and business rules, coordinate implementation and launch, and analyse what happened afterwards.

How I approached it

Start with the underlying customer problem

The need wasn’t simply “add reservations”. Customers who needed storage in the future had no good way to secure availability without starting a normal booking immediately.

Keep the first version focused

The first release had to prove whether customers actually wanted to reserve ahead before building every possible variation. The MVP focused on the core journey and the minimum business rules needed to make it operationally reliable. Where feasible, I prefer to test with a smaller sample before scaling.

Define success before launch

The project was to be measured after launch using reservation usage, reservation-to-booking conversion, commercial contribution, operational issues and customer behaviour.

Key decisions

  • Start with an MVPLaunch enough functionality to validate real customer demand before investing in every possible scenario.
  • Define the transition clearlyThe rules for when a reservation becomes a normal booking need to be explicit before implementation begins.
  • Protect real availabilityFuture reservations affect inventory, so availability behaviour needs to stay consistent for both immediate and future customers.
  • Keep the journey understandableThe customer should understand what they are reserving, what happens next and when the normal booking relationship begins.
  • Measure after launchDeployment is not the finish line. Usage, conversion, operational behaviour and commercial value decide whether the product deserves further investment.

Technical and operational complexity

Allowing a customer to reserve storage for a future date sounds simple, but it changes several connected parts of the product:

  • Future availability
  • Reservation state
  • Inventory allocation
  • Reservation-to-booking transition
  • Payment behaviour
  • Customer communication
  • Cancellation and expiry scenarios
  • Operational handling
  • Edge cases around timing and availability

The main product challenge was making sure the new reservation state worked consistently with the existing booking system, rather than creating a parallel process that operations could not manage.

Outcome

Using SQL, Metabase and PostHog, I analysed reservation usage, booking realisation, commercial contribution, booking behaviour, funnel performance and follow-up opportunities.

Post-launch analysis provided clear evidence that customers were using the feature, and allowed us to evaluate conversion, commercial contribution and where the journey should be improved next.

The feature also introduced an additional commercial mechanism while giving customers more flexibility over when their storage contract begins.

Product principleLaunch enough functionality to validate real demand before overbuilding the solution.
Product principleAn MVP should answer a business question, not simply ship fewer features.

What I learned

A feature is not successful because it shipped. The important question is what customers actually do afterwards, and whether that behaviour creates enough value to justify further investment.

This project reinforced the value of defining the measurement plan before launch, rather than deciding afterwards which numbers look interesting.
