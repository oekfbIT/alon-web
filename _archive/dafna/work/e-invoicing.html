Overview

European e-invoicing requirements are changing quickly and differ between countries. The product challenge isn’t simply “generate an invoice”.

How do we support structured invoice formats and country-specific rules without building a separate invoicing system for every market?

My role

I researched and defined requirements around EN 16931, Peppol, Peppol BIS Billing 3.0, UBL XML and ZUGFeRD, and country-specific requirements across markets including Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany and Austria.

How I approached it

Turning regulatory documentation into technical requirements, field mappings, system behaviour and implementation priorities, so compliance becomes something the product can actually do.

Key decisions

  • Design for changeRequirements keep changing by country, so the approach has to absorb new rules without a rebuild.
  • Compliance as product workRegulation is translated into fields, mappings and behaviour rather than left as a legal document.

Technical and operational complexity

The work sits between Finance, Accounting, Legal and compliance, Engineering and Product, and each brings different constraints to the same invoice.

Outcome

A requirements foundation for structured, multi-market e-invoicing that Engineering can build on and Finance can rely on.

What I learned

  • Regulatory work becomes manageable once it’s expressed as data, mappings and rules.
